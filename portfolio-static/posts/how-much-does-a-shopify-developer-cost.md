# How Much Does a Shopify Developer Cost in the UK?

Hiring a Shopify developer in the UK can cost anywhere from a small one-off fee for a simple theme tweak to several thousand pounds for a full store build or serious optimisation work.

That range is annoying when you are trying to budget, but it exists for a reason.

“Shopify developer” can mean very different things depending on what you need. One person might be changing button colours in a theme. Another might be rebuilding product pages, improving mobile conversion, fixing app bloat, integrating payments, setting up tracking, or building custom functionality that your theme cannot handle.

So the better question is not just:

“How much does a Shopify developer cost?”

It is:

“What type of Shopify work do I actually need, and what level of developer is worth paying for?”

## Typical Shopify developer rates in the UK

As a rough guide, UK Shopify developers often fall into these ranges:

- Junior or basic theme help: £25–£40 per hour
- Mid-level Shopify developer: £40–£75 per hour
- Specialist Shopify developer: £75–£120+ per hour
- Agency support: often £100–£200+ per hour equivalent

These are not fixed rules. You will find people cheaper and more expensive than this.

The important part is what you are actually buying.

A cheaper developer can be fine for small tasks like text changes, basic section edits or simple theme tweaks. But if the work affects checkout flow, conversion, performance, tracking or custom integrations, cheap can become expensive quickly.

Bad Shopify work usually does not look bad straight away. It shows up later as slow pages, broken mobile layouts, app conflicts, messy theme code, poor tracking or a store that becomes hard to maintain.

## Fixed price vs hourly Shopify work

Most Shopify work falls into either fixed-price projects or hourly support.

Fixed price usually makes sense when the scope is clear.

Examples:

- Build a landing page
- Create a custom product section
- Add a reviews block
- Redesign a homepage
- Set up a new Shopify store
- Improve a specific product page template

Hourly work makes more sense when the scope is uncertain.

Examples:

- Debugging theme issues
- Improving site speed
- Cleaning up app conflicts
- Fixing conversion problems
- Auditing checkout friction
- Investigating tracking issues
- Ongoing store improvements

The mistake many store owners make is forcing uncertain work into a fixed price.

That often creates rushed work, hidden assumptions, or developers padding the quote to protect themselves.

If you do not fully know what is wrong yet, start with an audit or a small diagnostic task before jumping into a full rebuild.

## Common Shopify project costs

Here are realistic ballpark ranges for common Shopify work.

### Small Shopify theme tweaks

Simple changes usually cost around:

**£50–£300**

This could include:

- Changing layout spacing
- Updating colours or typography
- Editing homepage sections
- Adjusting buttons
- Fixing small mobile layout issues
- Adding small content blocks

These jobs are usually straightforward if your theme is clean.

### Custom Shopify sections

Custom Liquid sections often cost around:

**£150–£600+**

This depends on complexity.

A simple trust badge section is much cheaper than a custom product comparison block, advanced filtering layout, custom upsell section or reusable landing page section.

### Shopify speed optimisation

Speed optimisation can range from:

**£300–£1,500+**

The cost depends on what is slowing the store down.

Common issues include:

- Oversized images
- Too many apps
- Render-blocking scripts
- Theme bloat
- Tracking scripts
- Page builder overhead
- Leftover code from uninstalled apps

Be careful with anyone promising a perfect PageSpeed score. The goal is not just a better score. The goal is a faster real buying experience, especially on mobile.

### Shopify conversion optimisation

Conversion-focused improvements often cost:

**£500–£2,500+**

This can include:

- Product page improvements
- Better add to cart visibility
- Mobile UX fixes
- Trust signal placement
- Cart drawer improvements
- Checkout flow review
- Delivery and returns messaging
- Review placement
- Better collection navigation

This is where specialist help is usually more valuable than cheap design work.

A store can look polished and still fail to convert. The goal is not just to make the site prettier. The goal is to make buying easier.

### Full Shopify store build

A full Shopify store build in the UK can range from:

**£1,500–£10,000+**

That is a wide range because “store build” can mean very different things.

A basic theme setup with light customisation may sit at the lower end.

A more serious build with custom design, conversion-focused product pages, app setup, tracking, SEO basics, speed work, email integration and launch support will cost more.

If someone quotes very low for a full store build, check exactly what is included.

You may be getting a lightly edited theme, not a properly planned ecommerce store.

### Custom Shopify functionality

Custom functionality can range from:

**£500–£5,000+**

Examples include:

- Custom product builders
- Advanced filtering
- Bundle logic
- Subscription setup
- Custom checkout-adjacent flows
- Third-party API integrations
- ERP or fulfilment integrations
- Custom dashboards
- Complex product options

This type of work needs proper scoping. Guessing the price too early is how projects go sideways.

## What affects the cost of a Shopify developer?

The biggest cost factors are not always obvious.

## The quality of your current theme

If your theme is clean, modern and not heavily hacked together, work is usually faster.

If your theme has years of patches, app leftovers and random custom code, even simple changes can take longer.

A developer may need to clean up or work around existing issues before making improvements.

## The number of apps installed

Apps are useful, but too many apps can make a store slower and harder to maintain.

If you have multiple apps affecting the same parts of the store — reviews, subscriptions, bundles, upsells, popups, tracking — changes become more delicate.

Sometimes the best development work is removing unnecessary complexity rather than adding more.

## How clear the scope is

Clear scope lowers cost.

Vague scope increases cost.

For example, this is vague:

“I want my store to look better and convert more.”

This is clearer:

“I want the product page improved on mobile, with delivery info, reviews, trust signals and add to cart visible earlier.”

The clearer the outcome, the easier it is to price properly.

## Whether the work affects conversion

Anything that affects the buying journey should be treated carefully.

This includes:

- Product pages
- Cart drawer
- Checkout path
- Navigation
- Collection pages
- Mobile layout
- Payment options
- Delivery messaging
- Reviews and trust signals

Small changes in these areas can affect revenue. That is not where you want rushed work.

## Whether integrations are involved

Shopify work becomes more expensive when it touches external systems.

Examples:

- Stripe
- PayPal
- Klaviyo
- Google Analytics
- Meta Pixel
- Fulfilment tools
- Zapier
- CRMs
- ERP systems
- Subscription platforms
- Review platforms

Integrations need testing because the issue is not just whether something appears on the page. It is whether data flows correctly.

## The level of strategic input needed

Some developers only implement what you ask for.

Others can look at the store and tell you what is actually worth fixing.

The second type costs more, but can save you money by stopping you from building the wrong thing.

If you already know exactly what you need, implementation help may be enough.

If your store is underperforming and you are not sure why, you probably need someone who understands ecommerce UX and conversion, not just code.

## When should you hire a Shopify developer?

You should consider hiring a Shopify developer if:

- Your store gets traffic but not enough sales
- Your mobile product pages feel clunky
- Your theme is hard to update
- Your site is slow because of app bloat
- You need custom sections your theme cannot handle
- Your checkout or cart flow creates friction
- Your tracking setup is unreliable
- You are relying on too many apps for simple features
- You are preparing for a launch and want the store built properly

A developer is not always the answer though.

If you have no traffic, no product-market fit and no idea who your customer is, paying for development may not fix the real problem.

In that case, focus on offer, traffic, product positioning and basic trust first.

## When should you avoid cheap Shopify development?

Cheap Shopify development is risky when the work touches revenue-critical areas.

Be careful with bargain quotes for:

- Speed optimisation
- Checkout fixes
- Tracking setup
- Theme rebuilds
- Product page redesigns
- Custom integrations
- Subscription setup
- App conflict fixes

That does not mean you should always choose the most expensive option.

It means you should understand what is being protected: your buying journey, your tracking accuracy, your store speed and your ability to maintain the store later.

A £100 fix can become expensive if it breaks mobile layouts, damages tracking or creates messy theme code that another developer has to clean up.

## What should you prepare before asking for a quote?

You will get a better quote if you prepare the right information.

Before contacting a Shopify developer, gather:

- Your store URL
- The theme you are using
- The apps installed
- The main problem you want solved
- Examples of stores you like
- Any known bugs or limitations
- Your budget range
- Your deadline
- Whether the work is urgent
- Access requirements, if known

For conversion or speed work, it also helps to share:

- Google Analytics access
- Search Console access
- Shopify analytics screenshots
- Heatmap or session recording insights
- Current conversion rate
- Top landing pages
- Best-selling products

You do not need all of this for a small task, but for serious optimisation it makes a big difference.

## How to tell if a Shopify developer is worth the price

A good Shopify developer should ask better questions than just:

“What colour do you want?”

For ecommerce work, they should care about:

- Mobile buying flow
- Product page clarity
- Checkout friction
- Store speed
- App bloat
- Tracking accuracy
- SEO basics
- Maintainability
- Customer trust
- How the store actually makes money

They should also be honest when a request is not worth building.

That matters.

A developer who says yes to everything may feel easy to work with at first, but they can also lead you into wasted spend.

## Is a Shopify agency worth it?

Sometimes, yes.

An agency may be useful if you need a larger team covering design, development, strategy, copywriting, SEO and ongoing support.

But for many small brands, a good specialist freelancer is often more practical.

You can usually move faster, communicate directly with the person doing the work, and avoid paying for unnecessary layers.

The trade-off is capacity. A freelancer may not be the right fit if you need a large multi-person delivery team or constant same-day support across many areas.

## So, how much should you budget?

For a serious small ecommerce brand, I would think about budgets like this:

- Small fixes: £50–£300
- Custom sections: £150–£600+
- Store audit: £150–£500+
- Speed optimisation: £300–£1,500+
- Conversion improvements: £500–£2,500+
- Full store build: £1,500–£10,000+
- Custom integrations: £500–£5,000+

These ranges are not perfect, but they are a useful starting point.

The main thing is to match the budget to the value of the problem.

If your store only makes a few sales a month, a £10,000 rebuild may be too much.

If your store already gets traffic and revenue, fixing conversion leaks can pay for itself much faster.

## Final thought

A Shopify developer should not just make your store look nicer.

The right developer should help make your store easier to buy from, easier to manage and less dependent on fragile workarounds.

If you are comparing prices, do not only ask:

“Who is cheaper?”

Ask:

“Who understands the buying journey, the technical risks and what this store actually needs to sell better?”

That is usually where the real value is.
